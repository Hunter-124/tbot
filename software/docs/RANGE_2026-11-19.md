# Range day 2026-11-19: what went wrong, and what changed

22 presses recorded (copies on the laptop in `scratch/range-2026-11-19/`, gitignored). One clay hit. Three fire
commands: one fired the gun (recoil 41.5 ms after the command, as calibrated), two showed NO RECOIL.

## 1. "It randomly stopped working": the Pi lost power, again and again

The run log shows no shutdown, no crash and no watchdog: the status lines just stop, and the next boot's clock is
back at ~09:25 (the Pi has no clock), so presses from different boots share names. From the presses' own uptimes
the Pi went through at least 6 boots between 09:25 and 09:28, several lasting only 1-2 minutes. Four presses are
files of zero bytes (power lost seconds after they were written). One boot (an hour up) had the firmware's
"under-voltage occurred" bit set (`throttled 0x50000`). The last real shot (recoil, 09:27:49) was followed by
a boot that died within 20 s. Software cannot cause a power cut; the supply chain can (pack charge, the lever
connector under recoil, the XL4015, the 20 AWG GPIO feed). **Check the hardware before the next outing.**

Changed:
- every recorded press is synced to the SD card when it is written (`os.sync`), so a power cut no longer leaves
  empty files;
- the log now says how long the Pi has been up at each start (a reboot is visible) and warns on the sticky
  "under-voltage happened since boot" bit, which catches dips shorter than the health poll.

## 2. The camera was dead for 16 minutes (08:56-09:12)

A hung camera process was killed; its shared-memory frame ring vanished with it, and every replacement failed on
the old name (`FileNotFoundError: /psm_...`, 45 respawns) until the service was restarted. Each replacement now
gets a fresh ring (`camera/proc.py`, test `test_respawn_after_the_frame_ring_vanished`; reproduced on the Pi
with the old code).

## 3. Video work moved off the gun

The console no longer renders clips or previews on the Pi while the pipeline runs. The laptop app (TBOT Console)
downloads each press's raw data (`frames.bin` is now served), renders clip + preview locally (H.264 through
Windows Media Foundation, ~0.5 s a press) and shows them in the console. Raw presses are kept in
`Videos\TBOT\<press>`.

## 4. Not picking up the clay the gun was on (trees behind it)

Traced on the presses where a real clay was followed (092512, 092521, 092601, 092733, 092749):

- **Wrong target.** The solver shot at the confirmed track with the most hits: a tree speck the scenery filter
  missed. Now: the moving track nearest the bore (`fire.target_nearest_bore`); tracks within `duplicate_deg`
  of it are the same clay and the one followed longest wins.
- **The clay never kept a track.** A one-frame track born on a speck took the clay's detection, got a velocity
  tens of deg/s wrong with full confidence, and rejected the clay from the next frame on; the clay's own new
  tracks found the table full of confirmed specks. Now: assignment by likelihood (d2 + ln det S, so a vague
  newborn does not outbid a good prediction), a clean twin track for a detection a newborn took
  (`tracker.twin_newborn`), a 1 deg gate floor for tracks under 8 hits (`tracker.young_gate_min_deg`,
  `young_hits`), 6 track slots, a tentative track seen twice survives a 2-frame dropout, and when the table is full
  a detection nearer the bore takes the place of the track farthest from it (never of an established moving
  track: fire.min_track_hits). On the recorded detections the clay
  had a confirmed track on it in 34/35 and 16/18 frames (before: 16/35 and 1/18).
- **Firing on a coasting track.** Never fire on a target missing from the last 2 frames (`fire.max_target_misses`).
- The detector loses a bright clay for a frame or two where it touches a light trunk (merged blob fails the shape
  test). Not changed yet; the tracker now rides through it.

Replayed through today's software with the gun's own lever (`tbot rescore`, which now takes the `actuator` section
from the press so the parked pre-travel delay is modelled):

| | this morning | now |
|---|---|---|
| presses that fire | 2 of 5 | 4 of 5 |
| scored shots that hit | 1 of 1 | 2 of 2 (092733: 2 cm off; 092512: 9 cm) |
| real miss, every frame with a solution, p50 | 6.4 deg | 2.4 deg |

`tbot vclay --clutter` (new) flies virtual clays through recorded swings WITH the detections the gun really made
in each frame: 16 % -> 43 % of clays hit on the tree/backyard presses; the clean sets are unchanged (69.4 %, 94.2 %).
(Those clutter presses include real birds and clays the gun followed, which the virtual clay is scored against, so
the absolute number is pessimistic; the replays of the real clays above are the better measure.)

## 5. Before the second outing (same afternoon)

All 18 readable presses of the morning went through the software again (`tbot rescore`), on the laptop and on
the Pi (same answers). With the fixes of section 4 only 4 of them fired. The other 14:

- **092521: the gun was held ON the clay for 2.5 s and never fired.** The clay showed as a 2-4 px sunlit glint
  against blue sky (later a 6-7 px dark sliver), so its size read 55-85 m and `fire.max_range_m` (40) refused
  116 frames in a row at P(hit) up to 0.89. A partly detected clay reads too far, never too near. Now
  (`fire.far_range_assume_m` 25, `far_range_sigma_m` 9, `far_range_limit_m` 120): a target reading beyond the
  window is solved at 25 m, known to 9 m, so the shot clears the bar only when the lead hardly depends on the
  range (a slow crossing, a going-away or incoming clay). The replay fires at P 0.54 and scores a hit (0.57 deg
  against 0.75). The gun now also shoots at anything small, moving and aimed at while the button is held (a
  bird at 80 m reads the same): the arming button is the shooter saying "this one".
- **13 presses of 0.4-1.9 s into trees** (092525 ... 092546): the canted gun looking into trunks and canopy, the
  nearest moving track 5-12 deg off the bore, P(hit) 0.00 throughout. Nothing to fire at; no clay visible in the
  frames. A clay in front of trees is not found by this detector: shoot it against the sky.

Holding the shot: the solver fired only at the top of P(hit), and 092749 waited 170 ms (7 frames) while P went
0.54 -> 0.98. A target in trees is not sure to be there for the promised frame. Now `fire.fire_now_p` 0.9 (at or
above it the shot goes at once) and `fire.max_hold_ms` 80 (the wait ends this long after the target first
cleared the bar). Virtual clays through the morning's swings at the 48 ms delay: 95.5 -> 95.2 % of clays hit,
quick shooter with 25 % dropouts 95.2 -> 95.2 %, with the morning's clutter 53.3 -> 54.3 % (0.8 instead of 0.9
costs a point; `hold_min_gain` 0.05 costs 1.2 and stays 0).

## 6. The ready light

An LED on GPIO26 (header pin 37) through 220-330 ohm to ground (pin 39, beside it in the same column),
`gpio.status_led_pin` (`hw/status_led.py`):

| light | meaning |
|---|---|
| flashing (once a second) | ready and LIVE, button up: frames and IMU fresh, the trigger is driven. Press and go |
| solid | the button is held: armed, live and ready, looking for its shot |
| a short blip every 2 s | ready, but a dry run or the test mode: nothing pulls the trigger (never solid, held or not) |
| flicker (5 a second) | the pipeline runs but is not ready: no fresh frame, IMU or attitude not there |
| off | booting, stopped, or no power |

The pipeline reports once a frame; the light's own thread shows "not ready" when the reports stop, so a hung frame
loop does not leave it flashing "ready". On the Pi: off for the ~10 s the pipeline takes to load after the service starts, then
flashing (live) or a blip every 2 s (dry); off again when the service stops. A process killed outright can leave the pin
as it was until the restart 3 s later claims it again.

## 7. Backyard, 15:18: it shot at scenery a fifth of a second after the press

Two fire commands in 11 presses, neither at a clay: a speck on the edge of a tree against the sky (track 5 frames /
110 ms old, moved 0.4 deg, P 0.57, read 19 m) and, indoors, the edge of a shadow on the ceiling (7 frames / 180 ms,
P 0.74, read 8 m). Both at 0.21-0.24 s after the button went down, on a track that had only just reached
`fire.min_track_hits` (5). Neither is the far-range rule of section 5 (both read inside 8-40 m).

Why: for a speck fixed in the world, "the bore will be on it in 48 ms" is a true firing solution whenever the
swinging bore passes over it. What stops that is the scenery filter, and it needs `detector.static_min_age_ms`
(100 ms) of history before it can call anything scenery; 5 hits take 100-170 ms at 30-50 fps, so a new speck could be
shot before the filter had its say. The morning's fire command on 092521 was the same thing (a track 80 ms old; the
clay was elsewhere), and so was the replayed shot on 092601.

Now `fire.min_track_age_ms` = 200: no shot at a target first seen less than 200 ms ago. All four of these would have
been refused. Virtual clays through the morning's swings: 95.2 -> 95.2 % of clays hit, hits per shot 96.6 -> 98.0 %;
a quick shooter with 25 % dropouts 95.2 -> 92.4 %. The four scoreable replays of real clays still fire and hit; 092601
no longer fires (its target was gone before the shot would have arrived).

Indoors stays a bad test: things 2-3 m away shift against each other as the gun turns (the filter assumes scenery
beyond `detector.static_min_range_m`, 6 m), so they look like movers however long they are followed.

`fire.min_travel_deg` (the target must have been seen this far from where it first appeared) was tried for the same
purpose and is left off: real swings on clays are 10-30 deg/s, so 1 deg already costs 2 points and 2 deg costs 9.

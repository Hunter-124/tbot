# Range day 2026-11-19: what went wrong, and what changed

22 presses recorded (copies on the laptop in `scratch/range-2026-11-19/`, gitignored). One clay hit. Three fire
commands: one fired the gun (recoil 41.5 ms after the command, as calibrated), two showed NO RECOIL.

## 1. "It randomly stopped working": the Pi lost power, again and again

The run log shows no shutdown, no crash and no watchdog: the status lines just stop, and the next boot's clock is
back at ~09:25 (the Pi has no clock), so presses from different boots share names. From the presses' own uptimes
the Pi went through at least 6 boots between 09:25 and 09:28, several lasting only 1-2 minutes. Four presses are
files of zero bytes (power lost seconds after they were written). One boot (an hour up) had the firmware's
"under-voltage occurred" bit set (`throttled 0x50000`). The last real shot (recoil, 09:27:49) was followed by
a boot that died within 20 s. Software cannot cause a power cut; the supply chain can (pack charge, the lever
connector under recoil, the XL4015, the 20 AWG GPIO feed). **Check the hardware before the next outing.**

Changed:
- every recorded press is synced to the SD card when it is written (`os.sync`), so a power cut no longer leaves
  empty files;
- the log now says how long the Pi has been up at each start (a reboot is visible) and warns on the sticky
  "under-voltage happened since boot" bit, which catches dips shorter than the health poll.

## 2. The camera was dead for 16 minutes (08:56-09:12)

A hung camera process was killed; its shared-memory frame ring vanished with it, and every replacement failed on
the old name (`FileNotFoundError: /psm_...`, 45 respawns) until the service was restarted. Each replacement now
gets a fresh ring (`camera/proc.py`, test `test_respawn_after_the_frame_ring_vanished`; reproduced on the Pi
with the old code).

## 3. Video work moved off the gun

The console no longer renders clips or previews on the Pi while the pipeline runs. The laptop app (TBOT Console)
downloads each press's raw data (`frames.bin` is now served), renders clip + preview locally (H.264 through
Windows Media Foundation, ~0.5 s a press) and shows them in the console. Raw presses are kept in
`Videos\TBOT\<press>`.

## 6. The ready light

An LED on GPIO26 (header pin 37) through 220-330 ohm to ground (pin 39, beside it in the same column),
`gpio.status_led_pin` (`hw/status_led.py`):

| light | meaning |
|---|---|
| flashing (once a second) | ready and LIVE, button up: frames and IMU fresh, the trigger is driven. Press and go |
| solid | the button is held: armed, live and ready, looking for its shot |
| a short blip every 2 s | ready, but a dry run or the test mode: nothing pulls the trigger (never solid, held or not) |
| flicker (5 a second) | the pipeline runs but is not ready: no fresh frame, IMU or attitude not there |
| off | booting, stopped, or no power |

The pipeline reports once a frame; the light's own thread shows "not ready" when the reports stop, so a hung frame
loop does not leave it flashing "ready". On the Pi: off for the ~10 s the pipeline takes to load after the service starts, then
flashing (live) or a blip every 2 s (dry); off again when the service stops. A process killed outright can leave the pin
as it was until the restart 3 s later claims it again.

## 7. Backyard, 15:18: it shot at scenery a fifth of a second after the press

Two fire commands in 11 presses, neither at a clay: a speck on the edge of a tree against the sky (track 5 frames /
110 ms old, moved 0.4 deg, P 0.57, read 19 m) and, indoors, the edge of a shadow on the ceiling (7 frames / 180 ms,
P 0.74, read 8 m). Both at 0.21-0.24 s after the button went down, on a track that had only just reached
`fire.min_track_hits` (5). Neither is the far-range rule of section 5 (both read inside 8-40 m).

Why: for a speck fixed in the world, "the bore will be on it in 48 ms" is a true firing solution whenever the
swinging bore passes over it. What stops that is the scenery filter, and it needs `detector.static_min_age_ms`
(100 ms) of history before it can call anything scenery; 5 hits take 100-170 ms at 30-50 fps, so a new speck could be
shot before the filter had its say. The morning's fire command on 092521 was the same thing (a track 80 ms old; the
clay was elsewhere), and so was the replayed shot on 092601.

Now `fire.min_track_age_ms` = 200: no shot at a target first seen less than 200 ms ago. All four of these would have
been refused. Virtual clays through the morning's swings: 95.2 -> 95.2 % of clays hit, hits per shot 96.6 -> 98.0 %;
a quick shooter with 25 % dropouts 95.2 -> 92.4 %. The four scoreable replays of real clays still fire and hit; 092601
no longer fires (its target was gone before the shot would have arrived).

Indoors stays a bad test: things 2-3 m away shift against each other as the gun turns (the filter assumes scenery
beyond `detector.static_min_range_m`, 6 m), so they look like movers however long they are followed.

`fire.min_travel_deg` (the target must have been seen this far from where it first appeared) was tried for the same
purpose and is left off: real swings on clays are 10-30 deg/s, so 1 deg already costs 2 points and 2 deg costs 9.

## 8. 15:35: the pre-travel was tripping the hammer (the break had moved 157 us)

Presses in the backyard "fired" with no fire command. The IMU shows it: a sharp 0.7-1.0 g knock 173-194 ms after the
button went down on every press with the hammer cocked (already there on the 14:15 bench presses, 0.7-1.75 g; not
in the morning, 0.33 g at most), and nothing after the later fire commands (0.04-0.17 g: the hammer was already down).
The servo settings were the morning's, so the mechanism had moved.

`tbot find-break` (new, tools/find_break.py) steps the lever in from max_us in 8 us steps and listens for the hammer
on the IMU: silent (0.01 g) down to 1260 us, **1.05 g at 1252 us**, confirmed by ear. The break was 1095 on the
11-18 bench: +157 us, about one tooth of the servo spline (14 deg). So the old pre-travel (1193) sat 59 us PAST the
break and the old rest (1340) most of the way into the pull.

`find-break --apply` moves rest / fire / min / break by the shift, which keeps the geometry the trigger delay was
measured with (rest 1497, pre-travel 60 % = 1350, break 1252, fire 1232, limits 1227-1500). Rest is now 3 us from
max_us: if the break moves further this way, refit the horn a tooth back instead.

Run it whenever the trigger set, the horn or the lever has been off the gun: EMPTY gun, hammer cocked,
`tbotctl stop`, `tbot find-break --unloaded --apply`, `tbotctl start live`.

**Trigger delay was 22 ms short.** Command -> ignition on the three live shots: 62.2 / 66.4 / 69.7 ms (the five dry
hammer knocks in the yard: 61-80 ms), against 44.5 ms assumed (38.5 by microphone on the morning's geometry + 6

**The double (160333).** The button was let go 72 ms after the command (10 ms after the recoil), the lever was sent

**Shots now reach the card.** logs/shots.jsonl: the fire event and that frame's full telemetry, appended and

## 11. Detection in a process of its own: built, measured, left switched off

Built as planned in section 10 (`camera.detect_in_process`, `tbot/vision/handover.py`, `tbot/camera/proc.py`):
a second process takes each frame, works out the attitude at the frame time from the IMU stream, runs the scenery
filter and the detector, and hands frame + detections + that attitude to the main loop, which tracks, solves,
fires and records. The tracks the scenery filter protects and looks for first go back the other way (their
filter states; the detector's side predicts them to its own frame time with the tracker's code). **It works and
it is faster, and it is not worth having on a Pi 3B: the default is off.**

**Not in the sensor process itself.** A range press replayed on the Pi with the camera and the IMU running in the
same process: detect 13 -> 36 ms, 22 frames a second. The capture and IMU threads hold the interpreter lock; that
is why the camera has its own process. So the detector has one too, between the two:
pipeline <- detector process <- sensor process (the detector process reads the sensor process exactly as the
pipeline used to, and replaces it when it dies, as before).

**Same decisions.** `tbot rescore --handover N` runs a press through the two processes with the scenery filter
N frames behind the single loop's. N = 0: every frame of the test press has the same detections, the same
tracks and the same decision as the single loop, P(hit) to six decimals (`tests/test_handover.py`; the attitude
differs in the 16th decimal because the two filters are fed the same samples in different portions). The single
loop itself is untouched: `tbot rescore` prints the same text before and after on the 9 presses of 2026-11-17
(8 shots) and on all 103 presses of 2026-11-19 on the Pi (14 shots, 13 of the 14 scored ones hits). Through the
hand-over with N = 0 the text is again the same on both sets. With N = 1 (one frame stale, as live) it is the
same on the 9 presses; on the 103 it is again 14 shots and 13 of 14 hits, but not the same 14: 153543 loses
its shot (a 0.71 deg hit at P 0.80), 155042 gains one (P 0.84, a hit), two shots go at another moment of the
same press and three are at a track with another number. That is what a frame of staleness in the scenery
filter does: other candidates measured in a cluttered frame, so other tracks.

**Bench** (`tbot cadence sessions/20261119-160243-press` on the Pi, service stopped; frames with the button
held, 4.6 detections and 5.9 tracks a frame; "age" here is frame taken -> solver done, without the camera):

## 12. Frame time, second cut (night): the detector's whole-frame steps in C, 22 -> 15 ms

Asked: is the threading right, is it built with -O3 and link-time optimisation. Measured on the Pi (service
stopped, `tbot bench --source replay:sessions/20261119-160243-press --frames 170 --no-overlay --no-write`):

- **OpenCV threads**: 4 (its default here) is the best setting. 1 thread 27.7 ms a frame, 2 threads 22.9, 3
  threads 22.0, 4 threads 21.3. Python threads stay ruled out (section 10), a third process too (section 11).
  The loop already freezes the garbage collector and shortens the interpreter's switch interval.
- **Compiler flags on the kernel as it was**: -O2, -O3, -O3 -mcpu=native and -O3 -mcpu=native -flto all gave
  21.3 ms. The kernel was per-candidate code, a tenth of the frame, and it is one C file, so there is nothing
  for link-time optimisation to join. The build is now -O3 with the CPU of the machine it is built on
  (`scripts/build_native.sh`; `TBOT_NATIVE_CPU=0` for a generic build) because the new whole-frame loops below
  are only vectorised at -O3 (the 4x4 box mean: 1.8 ms at -O2, 0.65 at -O3). Still no -ffast-math and no fused
  multiply-add: results may not depend on the optimiser.

Where the 22 ms went: 17 in the detector, of which OpenCV calls on the whole frame and the NumPy around them were
most. Moved into `tbot/vision/_measure.c` (OpenCV / NumPy stay as the reference and as the fallback):

| step | before | after | how |
|---|---|---|---|

Exactness. Components come out with OpenCV's numbers (its labels follow the first 2x2 block a component touches,
counted from the corner of the labelled rectangle), centroids bit for bit. OpenCV rounds a box mean half to even
in its own loop and half up for 2x2; `detector._area_rounding` finds out once per scale which this build does
and keeps `cv2.resize` if neither fits. A frame with more than 40000 runs or 8191 components goes to OpenCV.
`tests/test_native_front.py` compares all of it with OpenCV / the Python estimate on random images, on the
laptop and on the Pi.
